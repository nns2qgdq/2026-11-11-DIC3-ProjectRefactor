# Explainable Freeway Congestion Prediction Agent — IEEE-Style Abstract

## Abstract

This study aims to translate freeway speed predictions into evidence-based pre-trip information, addressing the gap between predictive accuracy and the effectiveness of travel recommendations. To mitigate the risk of misinformation generated by large language models (LLMs), an explainable congestion prediction agent is proposed that integrates forecasting tools with an official traffic regulation knowledge base. Numerical computations are performed programmatically, while the LLM is responsible for intent interpretation and natural language explanations. The system utilizes M05A gantry traffic data and focuses on ten mainline segments of Taiwan's National Freeway No. 5 to predict traffic speeds 30–60 minutes ahead. Numerical consistency checks and a template-based fallback mechanism are incorporated to improve response reliability. The forecasting models are trained on 2024 data and evaluated on 2025 data against persistence and historical-average baselines, using speed prediction errors and congestion detection performance as evaluation metrics. Preliminary results indicate lower prediction errors than the persistence baseline, although improvements during holiday periods remain limited and no clear advantage in congestion early-warning performance has been observed. Future work will assess response correctness and evidence traceability to determine the practical benefits of the proposed agent.

**Index Terms—** Congestion prediction, explainable artificial intelligence, intelligent transportation systems, large language models, traffic speed forecasting.
